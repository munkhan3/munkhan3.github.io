### The Formalities

We have {% include post-preview-link.html path="blogs/probability/some-measure-theory.md" text="talked before" %} about how we can use $$\sigma$$-algebras and filtrations to encode any particular agent's information. Suppose we are working together as one agent in a market of $$n$$ total agents, and the market is only awaiting our order (bid / ask). We can model the _value_ of the underlying product as a _filtered_ probability space...

$$
(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \ge 0}, \mathbb{P})
$$

Within this space, the $$k$$-th agent's information at time $$t$$ can be represented by an information set $$\mathcal{I}_t^{k}$$, which generates a sub-$$\sigma$$-algebra $$\mathcal{G}^{k}_t \subseteq \mathcal{F}_t$$.[^2] We assume that each agent performs an action determined by the following logic...

> The $$k$$-th agent makes a **single trading decision** at time $$t(k)$$, and the price of the product at that time is $$X_{t(k)}$$. The agent buys at the listed price if they expect the final price $$X_T$$ to be **at least the current price.[^3] Otherwise, they sell.** Their expectation is based on the **information they have** at that time, which is represented by their sub-$$\sigma$$-algebra $$\mathcal{G}_{t(k)}^k$$.

Formally, we can write this decision rule as...

$$
a(X_{t(k)})=
\begin{cases}
\textrm{Buy @ } X_{t(k)}, & \mathbb{E}[X_T \mid \mathcal{G}_{t(k)}^k] \geq X_{t(k)} \\
\textrm{Sell  @ } X_{t(k)}, & \mathbb{E}[X_T \mid \mathcal{G}_{t(k)}^k] < X_{t(k)}
\end{cases}
$$

We can then **represent the market as an aggregator of the information provided by its participants**. Suppose $$\mathcal{K}_t$$ is the set of agents that have participated in the market at time $$t$$. Then, if we assume for simplicity that an agent's decision perfectly communicates the information relevant to their forecast, we have...[^4]

$$
\require{mathtools}
\bigcup_{k \in \mathcal{K}_t} \mathcal{I}_t^{k} = \mathcal{I}_t^{mkt} \quad
\xrightarrow{\phantom{0}\sigma\phantom{0}} \quad
\mathcal{F}_t^{mkt} \coloneqq \bigvee_{k \in \mathcal{K}_t} \mathcal{G}_t^{k} \subseteq \mathcal{F}_t
$$

If we extend this idea a bit further, notice that we can view the market price at any time $$t$$ as a **_collective expectation_** -- the _expected final value of the product given all participant information_...

$$
X_t = \mathbb{E}\left[X_T\mid \mathcal{F}_t^{mkt}\right]
$$

Intuitively, this should make sense. If it doesn't, try thinking about it from the perspective of any individual agent looking to enter the market at a specific time. In some sense, the agent will trade if there is a _discrepancy_ between their expectation and the market price because they **expect to profit** based on their beliefs. This action drives the price towards their expectation as the market incorporates the new information revealed by the agent's actions. Since we can view the market at time $$t$$ as a collective that expects the final value of a product to be $$X_t$$, we can equate the current market price and the future market expectation.[^5]
