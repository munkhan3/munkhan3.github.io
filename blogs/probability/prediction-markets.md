<h3>Section Title</h3>

If you're a Duke fan and have strong opinions on whether the basketball team will win a championship this year, you can go to [Kalshi](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxmarmad/mens-college-basketball-tournament-champion/kxmarmad-26) and speculate on those opinions. Right now, Kalshi says the probability that Duke wins is ~20%. Based on that information, you can make an informed decision by following some straightforward logic:

1. If you think Duke has an incredible team and has a reasonable chance of winning (i.e., more than 20% chance), you might bet on `DUKE - YES`.
2. If you think Duke has a tendency to choke in the big games and are quite confident that they will not win (i.e., less than 20% chance), you might bet on `DUKE - NO`.[^1]
3. If you're just a fan of the game and don't have strong opinions either way (i.e., you agree there's around a 20% chance), it doesn't make too much sense to put your money on the line.[^2]

---

[^1]: Frankly, I think these odds are way too inflated. As a Duke fan, I have experienced too much heartbreak to get my hopes up again. Obviously, this is not financial advice.

[^2]: There are a few nuances to this process that we skip here. Some light reading: bid-ask spreads, uncertainty and confidence intervals, {% include post-preview-link.html path="blogs/game-theory/expected-utility.md" text="expected utility" %}.
